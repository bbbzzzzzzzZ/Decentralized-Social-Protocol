## I. Foundational Axioms of Algorithm Design

1. **Decentralization:** The system contains no administrative center with subjective task-assignment powers. The algorithm is solely responsible for "computation and public disclosure," possessing no right of mandatory requisition over any individual.
2. **Non-Fiat Credit:** The output regulated by the algorithm consists of **"Responsibility Credits / Dynamic Time Leverage,"** which can only be redeemed for experiential access to high-tier public resources, research computing power, and leisure time. They can never be converted into private ownership of the means of production or used for financial lending.
3. **Sovereignty Supremacy:** The registration of any capability does not equate to permanent standby availability. Every system prompt or notification is merely an "invitation/request," and individuals retain an absolute, penalty-free **Right of Refusal**.

## II. Module 1: Real-Time Gap Calculation Model

How does the system determine how many doctors, electricians, or sanitation workers society currently requires? Rather than relying on bureaucratic guesswork, dynamic indicators and safety thresholds automatically generate the **"Absolute Gap" ($G_i$)**.

### 2.1 Gap Calculation Formula

For any public maintenance domain $i$ (e.g., emergency medicine, power grid inspection, waste management), the real-time demand gap at time $t$, denoted as $G_i(t)$, is defined as:

$$G_i(t) = \max\left(0, \ D_i(t) - S_i(t)\right)$$

* **$D_i(t)$ (Rigid Demand Threshold):** A dynamic standard determined by baseline urban population data, historical disaster probabilities, WHO safety baselines, and physical wear-and-drop rates (e.g., required emergency medical staff per 10,000 residents).
* **$S_i(t)$ (Effective Supply):** The total volume of effective service capacity currently registered in that skill pool and in a "responsive status" for the current cycle.

### 2.2 Algorithmic Boundaries

* **Publicity Right:** The data hub broadcasts the status of $G_i(t)$ across the entire network in real-time.
* **No Assignment Authority:** When the algorithm detects $G_i(t) > 0$, it merely triggers a "Public Gap Alert" and automatically scales up the leverage multiplier for that domain, without ever directly forcing tasks upon any qualified citizen.

## III. Module 2: Dynamic Time Leverage Multiplier Model

To resolve the economic dilemma where "dirty, exhausting, high-threshold jobs find no takers," the system introduces the **Time Leverage Multiplier ($L_i$)**.

### 3.1 Core Variables Determining Leverage

The leverage multiplier $L_i$ is not a fixed value; it is dynamically determined by three core real-world variables:

1. **Training & Qualification Barrier Cost ($C_{\text{train}}$):** The time required to take a leave of absence for learning the skill, alongside peer-review certification difficulty.
2. **Physical/Psychological Burden & Risk Index ($R_{\text{risk}}$):** The degree of involvement regarding life safety, high-altitude operations, extreme environments, or high-intensity emotional labor.
3. **Real-Time Supply-Demand Ratio ($R_{\text{demand}}$):** The real-time ratio between the current gap $G_i(t)$ and the number of available responders.

| Task Tier | Typical Representatives | Qualification Barrier & Verification Method | Physical/Psychological Burden & Risk | Dynamic Leverage Multiplier ($L_i$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tier 1: High Barrier / High Risk** | Surgery, High-voltage grid repair, Disaster rescue | Extremely High (Long-term professional training, simulator retraining, and peer certification) | Extremely High (Involves life safety and major systemic risk) | **3.0x - 5.0x** (High leverage return; exchanges short working hours for massive leisure time) |
| **Tier 2: High Intensity / High Monotony** | Deep-well mining, Waste processing, Underground pipeline maintenance | Low to Medium (Focuses on operational standards and safety protection) | Extremely High (Physical exhaustion, harsh environments, high repetition) | **2.0x - 5.0x** (High leverage compensation) |
| **Tier 3: Routine Maintenance / Caregiving** | Community nursing, Routine public facility inspection, Basic education | Medium (Standardized skills and psychological competency assessment) | Medium (Emotional labor or regular physical labor) | **1.0x - 1.5x** (Standard return) |

## IV. Module 3: Decentralized Order-Grabbing & Surge Pricing Mechanism

### 4.1 Decentralized Skill Pool (Uber-Style Autonomous Matching)

* **User Terminal (App/Interface):** Users open the interface to view their personal "List of Verified Competencies" (e.g., Emergency Nursing Tier 1, Grid Inspection Tier 2).
* **Public Square:** The system displays real-time public demand gaps across surrounding communities alongside their corresponding time leverage multipliers (e.g., *"South Substation Grid Inspection: Gap of 2 personnel, Current Leverage 3.5x"*).
* **Autonomous Order-Grabbing:** Qualified individuals independently accept tasks based on their current time flexibility. Upon fulfilling the responsibilities, the system automatically credits their personal "Responsibility Credit Account" by multiplying the actual service duration by the current leverage multiplier.

### 4.2 Automated Surge Pricing & Circuit Breaker Mechanism

* **Time-Decay Auto-Surge:** If a public task receives no responses within time $T$ after publication (i.e., $G_i(t)$ fails to narrow), the algorithm automatically triggers tiered surge pricing, raising the leverage multiplier by 0.2x every 10 minutes until sufficient social response is attracted.
* **Physical Fallback & Circuit Breaker:** Under extreme circumstances (such as sudden disasters or continuous unfulfilled orders threatening safety baselines), the system automatically switches to the "Reserve Pool" or initiates Tier 4 basic rotation mechanisms for physical fallback handled by community temporary rotation squads.
